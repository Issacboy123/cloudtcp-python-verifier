#  MUTCD / CA MUTCD Zone-Geometry Rules

**Reading conventions:** STANDARD is mandatory; GUIDANCE is advisory; OPTION is permissive; SUPPORT remains informational. 

## 1\. Zone Components

### Z-001 — SUPPORT — TTC zone definition

A TTC zone is an area of a highway where road user conditions are changed because of a work zone, an incident zone, or a planned special event through the use of TTC devices, uniformed law enforcement officers, or other authorized personnel.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.02, para. 01; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### Z-002 — SUPPORT — Work zone boundaries

A work zone is a highway area with construction, maintenance, or utility work. It extends from the first warning sign or high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights on a vehicle to the END ROAD WORK sign or last TTC device.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.02, para. 02; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### Z-003 — SUPPORT — Incident zone boundaries

An incident zone is an area of a highway where temporary traffic controls are imposed by authorized officials in response to a traffic incident (see Section 6O.01). It extends from the first warning device (such as a sign, light, or cone) to the last TTC device or to a point where road users return to the original lane alignment and are clear of the incident.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.02, para. 03; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### Z-005 — SUPPORT — Four TTC zone components

As needed and based on engineering judgment, a TTC zone is often divided into advance warning, transition, activity, and termination areas (Figure 6B-1; §§6B.04–6B.07).

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.03, para. 01; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-006 — SUPPORT — Advance warning area definition

The advance warning area is the section of highway where road users are informed about the upcoming transition and activity areas or incident area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 01; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-007 — OPTION — Advance warning area forms

The advance warning area may vary from a single sign or high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights on a vehicle to a series of signs in advance of the TTC zone activity area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 02; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-008 — SUPPORT — Transition area definition

The transition area redirects road users out of their normal path and usually involves tapers.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.05, para. 01; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-009 — STANDARD — Required redirection through the transition

Except for mobile operations, when redirection of the road users’ normal path is required, road users shall be directed from the normal path to a new path with appropriate channelizing devices, traffic control devices, and/or TTC methods.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.05, para. 02; PDF p. 133, 134; printed p. 1223, 1224\.

### Z-010 — OPTION — Mobile operation transition alternative

Because it is impracticable in mobile operations to redirect the road users’ normal path with stationary channelization, more dominant vehicle-mounted traffic control devices, such as arrow boards, portable changeable message signs, and high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights, may be used instead of channelizing devices to establish a transition area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.05, para. 03; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-011 — SUPPORT — Activity area components

The activity area is the section of the highway where the work activity takes place. It is comprised of the work space, the traffic space, and the buffer space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 01; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-012 — SUPPORT — Work space definition and delineation

The work space is that portion of the highway closed to road users and set aside for workers, equipment, and material, and a shadow vehicle if one is used upstream. Work spaces are usually delineated for road users by channelizing devices or, to exclude vehicles and pedestrians, by temporary barriers.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 02; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-013 — OPTION — Stationary or moving work space

The work space may be stationary or may move as work progresses.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 03; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-014 — GUIDANCE — Separate work spaces within a project

Since there might be several work spaces (some even separated by several miles) within the project limits, each work space should be adequately signed to inform road users and reduce confusion.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 04; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-015 — SUPPORT — Traffic space definition

The traffic space is the portion of the highway in which road users are routed through the activity area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 05; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-016 — OPTION — Incident response staging space

When work occurs on a high-volume, highly-congested facility, a vehicle storage or staging space may be provided for incident response and emergency vehicles (for example, tow trucks and fire apparatus) so that these vehicles can respond quickly to road user incidents.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 16; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### Z-017 — SUPPORT — Termination area definition and limits

The termination area is the section of the highway where road users are returned to their normal driving path. The termination area extends from the downstream end of the work area to the last TTC device such as END ROAD WORK signs, if posted.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.07, para. 01; PDF p. 135; printed p. 1225\.

### Z-018 — OPTION — Signs announcing normal operations

An END ROAD WORK sign, a Speed Limit sign, or other signs may be used to inform road users that they can resume normal operations.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.07, para. 02; PDF p. 135; printed p. 1225\.

### Z-019 — GUIDANCE — Freeway and expressway advance warning extent

On freeways and expressways, advance warning sign placement should extend as far as ½ mile or more because drivers are conditioned to uninterrupted flow.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 03; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-020 — GUIDANCE — Urban nearest warning sign placement

On urban streets, the distance in feet from the TTC zone to the nearest warning sign should be 4 to 8 times the speed limit in mph. The high end should be used when speeds are relatively high.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 04; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-020-B — GUIDANCE — Higher-speed urban multiple-sign warning area

When two or more advance warning signs are used on higher-speed urban streets, such as major arterials, the advance warning area should extend a greater distance (Table 6B-1).

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 04; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-021 — OPTION — Single-sign short advance warning area

When a single advance warning sign is used (in cases such as low-speed residential streets), the advance warning area may be as short as 100 feet.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 05; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-022 — GUIDANCE — Rural first warning sign placement

On rural highways, the effective placement of the first warning sign, in feet, should be 8 to 12 times the speed limit in mph. The source wording “first warning sign” is retained; Table 6B-1 separately defines A, B, and C.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 06; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-022-B — GUIDANCE — Open-highway advance warning extent

For open-highway conditions, where two or more advance warning signs are normally used, the advance warning area should extend 1,500 feet or more (Table 6B-1).

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 06; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-023 — GUIDANCE — Advance warning spacing adjustments

The distances contained in Table 6B-1 are approximate, are intended for guidance purposes only, and should be applied with engineering judgment. These distances should be adjusted for field conditions, if necessary, by increasing or decreasing the recommended distances.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 07; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

### Z-025 — OPTION — Federal advance warning omission option

Advance warning may be eliminated when the activity area is sufficiently removed from the road users’ path so that it does not interfere with the normal flow.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04, para. 09; PDF p. 133; printed p. 1223\.

**Reviewer note:** Federal baseline retained for comparison; California modifies the omission conditions in CA-001.

### Z-026 — GUIDANCE — Federal baseline — advance warning sign spacing

The following sign spacings should be used, subject to engineering judgment and field adjustment; they are not universal mandatory minima. The highway agency or site-road owner determines the federal low/high speed category.

| Roadway | A (ft) | B (ft) | C (ft) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Urban, low speed | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Urban, high speed | 350 | 350 | 350 |
| Rural | 500 | 500 | 500 |
| Expressway/freeway | 1,000 | 1,500 | 2,640 |

A is the distance from the transition or point of restriction to the nearest sign; B is from the nearest to the second sign; C is from the second to the third sign. In this table, “first” means closest to the TTC zone and “third” means farthest upstream. California replaces the speed categories and adds rows in CA-005.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.04 para. 07; Table 6B-1; §6H.02 para. 01; PDF p. 133, 140, 171; printed p. 1223, 1230, 1261\.

### Z-027 — SUPPORT — Component geometry in Figure 6B-1

The figure places advance warning upstream of transition, then activity, then termination. The work space and adjacent traffic space are within the activity area. An upstream longitudinal buffer lies between transition and work space; a lateral buffer separates work and traffic spaces. A downstream longitudinal buffer is shown between work space and downstream taper in termination. A shoulder taper is shown upstream of the principal transition.

Longitudinal means along road-user flow; lateral means across it. This is a component illustration, not a requirement to provide every optional buffer; apply §§6B.06–6B.08. California adds references to Tables 6B-2(CA) and 6B-3(CA).

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.03 para. 01; Figure 6B-1; PDF p. 133, 137; printed p. 1223, 1227\.

### Z-028 — GUIDANCE — Position of advance warning signs

Where highway conditions permit, warning signs should be placed in advance of the transition and activity areas at varying distances depending on roadway type, condition, and posted speed. Table 6B-1 contains information regarding the spacing of advance warning signs. Where a series of two or more advance warning signs is used, the closest sign to the transition and activity areas should be placed approximately 100 feet for low-speed urban streets to 1,000 feet or more for freeways and expressways.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6H.02, para. 01; PDF p. 171; printed p. 1261\.

### Z-029 — GUIDANCE — Crossroad and driveway warning

Where traffic can enter a TTC zone from a crossroad or a major (high-volume) driveway, an advance warning sign should be used on the crossroad or major driveway.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6H.03, para. 02; PDF p. 172; printed p. 1262\.

### Z-030 — GUIDANCE — Termination sign location

When used, the END ROAD WORK (G20-2) sign (see Figure 6H-1) should be placed near the downstream end of the termination area, as determined by engineering judgment.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6H.36, para. 01; PDF p. 180; printed p. 1270\.

### Z-031 — SUPPORT — Warning area movement in mobile operations

Mobile operations also include work activities where workers and equipment move along the road without stopping, usually at slow speeds. The advance warning area moves with the work area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.01, para. 15; PDF p. 237; printed p. 1327\.

### Z-032 — SUPPORT — Work beyond the shoulder and warning threshold

When work is being performed beyond the shoulders, but within the right-of-way, little or no TTC might be needed. TTC generally is not needed where work is confined to an area 15 feet or more from the edge of the traveled way. However, TTC is appropriate where distracting situations exist, such as vehicles parked on the shoulder, vehicles accessing the worksite via the highway, and equipment traveling on or crossing the roadway to perform the work operations (for example, mowing). A typical application for work beyond the shoulder is shown in Figure 6P-1.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.05, para. 01; PDF p. 240; printed p. 1330\.

### Z-033 — STANDARD — Wide paved shoulder — advance warning

For short-term through long-term stationary operations, when a paved shoulder 8 feet or more wide is closed, at least one advance warning sign shall be used.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.06, para. 02; PDF p. 240; printed p. 1330\.

### Z-033-B — STANDARD — Wide paved shoulder — advance channelization

For short-term through long-term stationary operations, when a paved shoulder 8 feet or more wide is closed, channelizing devices shall close the shoulder in advance, delineate the beginning of the work space, and direct traffic to remain within the traveled way.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.06, para. 02; PDF p. 240; printed p. 1330\.

### Z-034 — GUIDANCE — Analyze partial-lane occupation

When work occupies part of a lane, traffic volume, vehicle mix (buses, trucks, cars, and bicycles), speed, and capacity should be analyzed to determine whether to close the affected lane.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.07, para. 02; PDF p. 241; printed p. 1331\.

### Z-034-B — GUIDANCE — Remaining lane width with encroachment

When work occupies part of a lane, the lane should be closed unless the encroachment permits a remaining lane width of 10 feet. See the conditional 9-foot option Z-036 and California restriction CA-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.07, para. 02; PDF p. 241; printed p. 1331\.

### Z-035 — GUIDANCE — Truck off-tracking and minimum lane width

Truck off-tracking should be considered when determining whether the minimum lane width of 10 feet is adequate.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.07, para. 03; PDF p. 241; printed p. 1331 / Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-43, TA-43, note 1; PDF p. 348; printed p. 1438\.

### Z-036 — OPTION — Federal reduced lane width option

A lane width of 9 feet may be used for short-term stationary work on low-volume, low-speed roadways when vehicular traffic does not include longer and wider heavy commercial vehicles.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.07, para. 04; PDF p. 241; printed p. 1331\.

**Reviewer note:** Federal baseline retained for comparison; California narrows the option in CA-009.

### Z-037 — GUIDANCE — Median work proximity threshold

If work in the median of a divided highway is within 15 feet from the edge of the traveled way for either direction of travel, TTC should be used through the use of advance warning signs and channelizing devices.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.08, para. 02; PDF p. 241; printed p. 1331\.

### Z-038 — OPTION — Additional interior-lane closure for lateral clearance

For work on a multi-lane, non-access-controlled road, when closing a left-hand lane on a multi-lane undivided road, as vehicular traffic flow permits, the two interior lanes may be closed, as shown in Figure 6P-30, to provide drivers and workers additional lateral clearance and to provide access to the work space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.11, para. 09; PDF p. 243; printed p. 1333\.

### Z-039 — STANDARD — Centerline delineation for left-lane closure

For work on a multi-lane, non-access-controlled road, when only the left-hand lane is closed on undivided roads, channelizing devices shall be placed along the center line as well as along the adjacent lane.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.11, para. 10; PDF p. 243; printed p. 1333\.

### Z-040 — GUIDANCE — Adjacent lane closure consideration

For work on a multi-lane, non-access-controlled road, when an interior lane is closed, an adjacent lane should also be considered for closure to provide additional space for vehicles and materials and to facilitate the movement of equipment within the work space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.11, para. 11; PDF p. 243; printed p. 1333\.

### Z-041 — GUIDANCE — Intersection cross-street warning

For work at an intersection, advance warning signs, devices, and markings should be used on all cross streets, as appropriate.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.12, para. 06; PDF p. 244; printed p. 1334\.

### Z-041-B — GUIDANCE — Intersection warning above 40 mph

For intersection work, additional advance warning signs should be used when the posted speed limit, pre-work off-peak 85th-percentile speed, or anticipated speed exceeds 40 mph. The typical applications depict urban arterial intersections.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.12, para. 06; PDF p. 244; printed p. 1334\.

### Z-042 — OPTION — Restricted near-side advance warning space

Where space is restricted in advance of near-side work spaces, as with short block spacings, two warning signs may be used in the advance warning area, and a third action-type warning or a regulatory sign (such as Keep Left) may be placed within the transition area.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.12, para. 10; PDF p. 244; printed p. 1334\.

### Z-043 — GUIDANCE — Far-side lane closure starts before intersection

When a lane through an intersection must be closed on the far side, it should also be closed on the near-side approach to preclude merging movements within the intersection.

Z-044 (§6N.12 para. 13\) permits a near-side mandatory turn lane where significant turns occur.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.12, para. 12; PDF p. 244; printed p. 1334\.

### Z-044 — OPTION — Near-side mandatory turn lane option

If there are a significant number of vehicles turning from a near-side lane that is closed on the far side, the near-side lane may be converted to a mandatory turn lane.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.12, para. 13; PDF p. 244; printed p. 1334\.

### Z-045 — GUIDANCE — Assess freeway and expressway queues

For lane closures on freeways or expressways that may create queues, expected queue length should be assessed during TTC plan design.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.13, para. 03; PDF p. 245; printed p. 1335\.

### Z-045-B — GUIDANCE — Adjust freeway and expressway warning for queues

For lane closures on freeways or expressways that may create queues, adjustments to sign spacing and sign count, and use of more conspicuous devices, should be considered to increase the advance warning area’s distance and conspicuity.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.13, para. 03; PDF p. 245; printed p. 1335\.

### Z-046 — STANDARD — Separate opposing traffic on a divided-roadway closure

When two-lane, two-way traffic is maintained on one roadway of a normally divided highway, opposing traffic shall be separated throughout the two-way operation by temporary traffic barriers (concrete safety-shape or approved alternate), channelizing devices, Narrow Two-Way Traffic (W6-4) signs on flexible supports (§6H.17), or a temporary raised island.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.14, para. 02; PDF p. 245, 246; printed p. 1335, 1336\.

### Z-046-B — STANDARD — Signing and markings alone do not provide separation

When two-lane, two-way traffic is maintained on one roadway of a normally divided highway, markings and complementary signing shall not be used by themselves for opposing-flow separation.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.14, para. 02; PDF p. 245, 246; printed p. 1335, 1336\.

### Z-047 — STANDARD — Prevent queues across tracks

Where a grade crossing is within or near a TTC zone, lane restrictions, flagging, or other operations shall not create conditions that queue vehicles across the tracks.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.17, para. 01; PDF p. 246; printed p. 1336 / §8A.13; PDF pp. 377–378; Figure 6P-46; PDF p. 355\.

### Z-047-B — STANDARD — Control unavoidable track-crossing queues

If queuing across tracks cannot be avoided at a grade crossing within or near a TTC zone, a uniformed law enforcement officer or flagger shall be provided at the crossing to prevent vehicles from stopping on the tracks, even when automatic warning devices are present.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.17, para. 01; PDF p. 246; printed p. 1336 / §8A.13; PDF pp. 377–378; Figure 6P-46; PDF p. 355\.

### Z-048 — OPTION — TA-1 warning omission offset

In TA-1, the ROAD WORK AHEAD sign or Workers (W21-1a) sign may be omitted where the work space is behind a barrier, more than 24 inches behind the curb, or 15 feet or more from the edge of any roadway.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-1, TA-1, note 3; PDF p. 260; printed p. 1350\.

### Z-049 — STANDARD — TA-3 wide shoulder advance warning

In TA-3, when a paved shoulder 8 feet or more wide is closed, at least one advance warning sign shall be used.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-3, TA-3, note 8; PDF p. 264; printed p. 1354\.

### Z-049-B — STANDARD — TA-3 wide shoulder channelization

In TA-3, when a paved shoulder 8 feet or more wide is closed, channelizing devices shall close the shoulder in advance, delineate the beginning of the work space, and direct vehicular traffic to remain within the traveled way.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-3, TA-3, note 8; PDF p. 264; printed p. 1354\.

### Z-050 — GUIDANCE — TA-4 stationary warning distance

In TA-4, in those situations where multiple work locations within a limited distance make it practicable to place stationary signs, the distance between the advance warning sign and the work should not exceed 5 miles.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-4, TA-4, note 1; PDF p. 266; printed p. 1356\.

### Z-051 — GUIDANCE — TA-4 distance plaque range

In TA-4, in those situations where the distance between the advance signs and the work is 2 miles to 5 miles, a Supplemental Distance plaque should be used with the ROAD WORK AHEAD (W20-1) sign.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-4, TA-4, note 2; PDF p. 266; printed p. 1356\.

**Reviewer note:** The extraction identifies a California-added SHOULDER WORK AHEAD (C24(CA)) sign alternative; the 2-to-5-mile distance is unchanged. No separate classification for that added permission is supplied.

### Z-052 — GUIDANCE — TA-6 lane width and measuring points

In TA-6, all lanes should be a minimum of 10 feet in width as measured to the near face of the channelizing devices.

Read the conditional 9-foot option Z-056 and California State-highway exclusion CA-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-6, TA-6, note 1; PDF p. 270; printed p. 1360\.

### Z-053 — GUIDANCE — TA-15 lane width and measuring points

In TA-15, the lanes on either side of the center work space should have a minimum width of 10 feet as measured from the near edge of the channelizing devices to the edge of the pavement or the outside edge of the paved shoulder.

Read the conditional 9-foot option Z-057 and California State-highway exclusion CA-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-15, TA-15, note 1; PDF p. 289; printed p. 1379 / Federal MUTCD §6P.01 para. 02; Figure 6P-15 (TA-15); PDF p. 290; printed p. 1380\.

### Z-054 — GUIDANCE — TA-16 lane width and measuring points

In TA-16, the lanes on either side of the center work space should have a minimum width of 10 feet as measured from the near edge of the channelizing devices to the edge of the pavement or the outside edge of the paved shoulder.

Read any applicable reduced-width option with the California State-highway exclusion CA-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-16, TA-16, note 1; PDF p. 291; printed p. 1381 / Federal MUTCD §6P.01 para. 02; Figure 6P-16 (TA-16); PDF p. 292; printed p. 1382\.

### Z-055 — GUIDANCE — TA-26 lane width and measuring points

In TA-26, all lanes should be a minimum of 10 feet in width as measured to the near face of the channelizing devices.

Read the conditional 9-foot option Z-058 and California State-highway exclusion CA-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-26, TA-26, note 1; PDF p. 313; printed p. 1403 / Federal MUTCD §6P.01 para. 02; Figure 6P-26 (TA-26); PDF p. 314; printed p. 1404\.

### Z-056 — OPTION — TA-6 nine-foot lane option

In TA-6, for short-term use on low-volume, low-speed roadways with vehicular traffic that does not include longer and wider heavy commercial vehicles, a minimum lane width of 9 feet may be used.

### Z-057 — OPTION — TA-15 nine-foot lane option

In TA-15, a lane width of 9 feet may be used for short-term stationary work on low-volume, low-speed roadways when motor vehicle traffic does not include longer and wider heavy commercial vehicles.

California §6N.07 para. 04 separately excludes State highways (CA-009).

### Z-058 — OPTION — TA-26 nine-foot lane option

In TA-26, for short-term use on low-volume, low-speed roadways with vehicular traffic that does not include longer and wider heavy commercial vehicles, a minimum lane width of 9 feet may be used.

### Z-059 — GUIDANCE — TA-6 speed-dependent treatment

The TA-6 treatment should be used on a minor road with low speeds; for higher-speed traffic conditions, a lane closure should be used. No numerical speed threshold is supplied.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-6, TA-6, note 2; PDF p. 270; printed p. 1360\.

### Z-060 — OPTION — TA-6 shift onto suitable opposite shoulder

In TA-6, where the opposite shoulder is suitable for carrying vehicular traffic and of adequate width, lanes may be shifted by use of closely-spaced channelizing devices, provided that the minimum lane width of 10 feet is maintained.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01; notes to Figure 6P-6, TA-6, note 5; PDF p. 270; printed p. 1360\.

### Z-061 — GUIDANCE — TA-12 warning sign alignment

In TA-12, warning sign locations should be adjusted as needed for horizontal or vertical roadway alignment. The depicted sign spacings are minimums for this application.

### Z-061-B — GUIDANCE — TA-12 signal-head height

In TA-12, signal-head heights should be adjusted as needed to conform to vertical alignment.

### Z-062 — SUPPORT — TA-12 maximum activity-area length

In TA-12, the maximum length of activity area for one-way operation under temporary traffic control signal control is determined by the capacity required to handle the peak demand.

### Z-067 — STANDARD — TA-18 limitation to low-speed low-volume streets

In TA-18, this TTC shall be used only for low-speed facilities having low traffic volumes.

## 3\. Buffer Spaces

### B-001 — SUPPORT — Buffer space definition

The buffer space is a lateral and/or longitudinal area that separates road user flow from the work space or an unsafe area, and might provide some recovery space for an errant vehicle.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 06; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-002 — GUIDANCE — Keep buffer space free of work and storage

Neither work activity nor storage of equipment, vehicles, or material should occur within a buffer space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 07; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-003 — OPTION — Longitudinal and lateral buffer placement

Buffer spaces may be positioned either longitudinally or laterally with respect to the direction of road user flow. The activity area may contain one or more lateral or longitudinal buffer spaces.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 08; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-004 — OPTION — Upstream longitudinal buffer option

A longitudinal buffer space may be placed in advance of a work space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 09; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-005 — SUPPORT — Buffer delineation

Typically, the buffer space is formed as a traffic island and defined by channelizing devices.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 12; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-006 — SUPPORT — Buffer boundary when a vehicle or sign occupies a closed lane

When a shadow vehicle, arrow board, or changeable message sign is placed in a closed lane in advance of a work space, only the area upstream of the vehicle, arrow board, or changeable message sign constitutes the buffer space.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 13; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-007 — OPTION — Lateral buffer uses

The lateral buffer space may be used to separate the traffic space from the work space, as shown in Figures 6B-1 and 6B-2, or such areas as excavations or pavement-edge drop-offs. A lateral buffer space also may be used between two travel lanes, especially those carrying opposing flows.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 14; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

### B-008 — GUIDANCE — Lateral buffer width

The width of a lateral buffer space should be determined by engineering judgment.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.06, para. 15; PDF p. 134; printed p. 1224\.

**Reviewer note:** No numerical lateral-buffer width is supplied; engineering judgment is the stated selection method.

## 5\. Necessary Interpretation / Dependency Rules

### D-001 — STANDARD — Meaning of STANDARD

STANDARD states required, mandatory or specifically prohibitive practice; generally uses “shall.” In limited location-specific cases, a documented engineering study might indicate a deviation is appropriate. Standards can be modified by Options.

A specified documented engineering study is distinct from ordinary engineering judgment.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1C.01 para. 01A; PDF p. 12; printed p. 85\.

### D-002 — STANDARD — Meaning of GUIDANCE

GUIDANCE states recommended practice in typical situations, with deviations allowed if engineering judgment or engineering study indicates appropriateness; generally uses “should.” Guidance can be modified by Options.

This definition is STANDARD; the provisions it defines remain GUIDANCE.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1C.01 para. 01B; PDF p. 12; printed p. 85\.

### D-003 — STANDARD — Meaning of OPTION

OPTION states permissive practice and carries no requirement or recommendation; generally uses “may.” It can modify a Standard or Guidance provision.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1C.01 para. 01C; PDF p. 12; printed p. 85\.

### D-004 — STANDARD — Meaning of SUPPORT

SUPPORT is informational and conveys no mandate, recommendation, authorization, prohibition or enforceable condition.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1C.01 para. 01D; PDF p. 12; printed p. 85\.

### D-005 — SUPPORT — Figure and table classification

Figures and tables supplement text and might constitute Standard, Guidance, Option or Support. Refer to the appropriate text to classify the figure, table or note.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1A.04 para. 05; PDF p. 2; printed p. 57\.

### D-006 — GUIDANCE — Example numerals printed on device images

Except where the section text requires or recommends a specific numeral, numerals displayed on device images should be regarded as examples and changed to fit the installation.

This does not erase dimension arrows or specific TA notes.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §1A.04 para. 06; PDF p. 2; printed p. 57\.

### D-007 — SUPPORT — Typical application classification

In general, the procedures illustrated represent minimum solutions for the situations depicted. Except for the notes (which are clearly classified using headings as being Standard, Guidance, Option, or Support), the information presented in the typical applications can generally be regarded as Guidance.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01, para. 02; PDF p. 256; printed p. 1346\.

### D-008 — OPTION — Typical application site or jurisdiction deviations

TTC plans may deviate from the typical applications described in this Chapter to allow for conditions and requirements of a particular site or jurisdiction.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01, para. 03; PDF p. 256; printed p. 1346\.

### D-009 — OPTION — Supplement or adjust TA devices

Devices may be added to a typical application, and device spacing may be adjusted, to provide additional reaction time or delineation.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01, para. 04; PDF p. 256; printed p. 1346 / Federal MUTCD §6B.01, para. 24; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### D-009-B — OPTION — Reduce TA devices for field conditions

Fewer devices than shown in a typical application may be used based on field conditions.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6P.01, para. 04; PDF p. 256; printed p. 1346 / Federal MUTCD §6B.01, para. 24; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### D-010 — GUIDANCE — Alter typical applications to actual conditions

Typical applications should be altered, when necessary, to fit the conditions of a particular TTC zone.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.01, para. 23; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### D-011 — OPTION — Increase warning spacing and taper length

Sign spacings and taper lengths in typical applications may be increased to provide additional driver-response time or space. The duplicate device-supplement permission is retained in D-009.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.01, para. 24; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### D-012 — OPTION — Omit optional typical-application devices

Devices labeled as optional in the typical applications may be deleted.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6B.01, para. 25; PDF p. 132; printed p. 1222\.

### D-013 — STANDARD — Long-term stationary work

Long-term stationary shall mean work occupying a location more than 3 days.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.01 para. 02A-E; PDF p. 236; printed p. 1326\.

Intermediate-term stationary shall mean work occupying a location more than one daylight period up to 3 days, or nighttime work lasting more than 1 hour.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.01 para. 02A-E; PDF p. 236; printed p. 1326\.

**Citation:** Federal MUTCD §6N.01 para. 02A-E; PDF p. 236; printed p. 1326\.
